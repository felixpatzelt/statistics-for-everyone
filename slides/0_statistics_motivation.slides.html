Data ❤︎ Statistics ❤︎ AI¶

  • You want to make better decisions?
  • You want to understand what generative AI does?
  • You want to work with data?

A bit of theory will help you - even if you didn't like math in the past!

Let's find out why!

Intuitive decision making can lead you astray¶

  • B. F. Skinner showed that rewards reinforce behaviours.
  • Hence, animals can be trained to do many tasks
  • Randomly rewarding pigeons reinforced
    apparently random behaviours ("superstitions").

Reasoning and discussing isn't always enough¶

  • Participants in the TV show Trick or Treat (S2/E6) had to find out how to score points.
  • They developed complex ideas on how using various available objects scored points

Humans detect patterns where there are none¶

In the show, points increased every time a fish swam across an aquarium

"We got to a hundred points, so we obviously did it right." - A participant.

Focusing on spurious signals can be costly¶

  • Participants missed actual hints that would have allowed them
    to win more money more easily
  • See part 1 and part 2

Even quantitative data can mislead without proper analysis¶

See also: Spurious correlations, Why not to use two axes

Algorithms can make similar mistakes¶

  • One common problem in machine learning is over- or underfitting variations in training data.
  • The resulting models don't generalise well to future observations.

Modern AI models inherit human biases¶

  • They (e.g. ChatGPT) learn to extract statistical patterns from vast amounts of text
  • Reproduce and even overemphasize biases in their training data or guardrails.
  • In 2025 still become unreliable where they had little training data

Statistical uncertainty can help to tell the signal from the noise¶


For example:

  • In 2016, many were certain that Clinton would win the US election.
  • On Nov. 4, four days before the election, a statistics website reported:
    "Trump Is Just A Normal Polling Error Behind Clinton"
